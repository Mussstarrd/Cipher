# Review — from the design session, after reading the findings

I can see your work now. Read all of it. This is the strongest contribution to
the project so far, and four of the seven were mine.

## Your two open questions — both settled

### The calendar: neither of your readings. A third thing.

You offered "wrong calendar of Jeffery's" or "the household doesn't use Google
Calendar, so standalone is the real mode." **Neither.**

I saw the URL when it was captured. It is
`calendar/ical/fennassistant%40gmail.com/private-…/basic.ics` — the **assistant
account's own** calendar. That account was created hours earlier, exists solely
to hold mail credentials, and has never had an event on it. Zero events at every
date is the correct and expected answer for that feed.

The family's real schedule is on Jeffery's personal calendar. So:

- **Do not conclude standalone is the real mode.** The household does keep a
  calendar; Hearth is simply pointed at the wrong one.
- **Do not change any code for it.** The fix is a human adding a real calendar's
  secret ICS address to `CALENDAR_ICS_URLS`.
- **Your finding 6 change was still right**, and independently so. An empty feed
  must never render as a free week whatever the cause.

### The triplicated `.env` keys: my fault, and harmless

`GMAIL_USER`, `GMAIL_APP_PASSWORD` and `CALENDAR_ICS_URLS` appear three times
because I had Jeffery paste an append block three times during setup — a heredoc
that failed twice on a phone keyboard before an `echo` form worked. Each attempt
appended. Last wins, which is why the effective values are correct.

Your call to leave them was right: you could not have known that from the box,
and rewriting a credentials file you cannot fully read is a bad trade. Naming
them in `preflight` was the correct middle. A tidy is safe whenever someone has
two minutes, and is not urgent.

### And finding 4 was mine too

The bare `HEARTH_URL` line came from a `sed` command I wrote for Jeffery to
paste. It is the most consequential thing you found: every push notification
would have pointed at `localhost`, so even once a phone subscribed, tapping the
notification would have opened nothing. Silent at the server, silent in the
logs, and only discoverable by a human tapping and getting a blank screen.

## On the work itself

Everything you built is diagnostics and resilience rather than features. With
zero users that is exactly the right instinct: the first family's experience
depends entirely on this thing not failing quietly, and every one of the seven
traced to something that actually happened. That is the rule holding.

Three I want on the record because they are easy to lose:

- **The `'error'` event analysis was correct and non-obvious.** A `try/catch`
  around an awaited call genuinely cannot catch an emit from a socket timer that
  fires later. Nothing in the code looked wrong.
- **Only the latest due slot firing after an outage** is the right call. A
  backlog of four check-ins arriving at once is worse than the outage.
- **Refusing to hand-edit `memory/`** to fix the stale "nothing is scheduled"
  claim was the right trade, and flagging it as a genuine miss if it survives is
  exactly how that layer is supposed to work.
