Ground rules baked into every phrase below: no artist or song names; the p-word for the keyboard appears at most twice in the whole bank; no stock mallet presets; no FX-style language (the client-banned terms are absent from every phrase); glitch is written as rhythm (stutter-repeats, half-beat silences, retriggered snares, bit-crushed stabs); nothing vocal. ## 1. Radio reality check (2026)

- **The chart is a rap-and-R&B chart again.** Hip-hop held roughly a fifth of Hot 100 top-10 slots in the first half of 2026 (the bulk from one May album that dropped nine top-10s at once), and the year's Hot R&B/Hip-Hop number ones split between melodic-rap radio records and slow, dark R&B ballads. The radio instrumental that wins now is either a *mid-tempo melodic trap record with a memorable 4-bar loop* or a *sub-70 BPM R&B record with a real chord movement*. Nothing in between is charting.
- **BPM by sub-lane (as heard, double-time hats implied):** melodic trap 130–150 (felt 65–75); hard/rage-adjacent trap 140–160; NY drill and "sexy drill" 138–146 with the sliding-808 grid; Detroit/Flint 90–105 straight (no half-time, the bass IS the drums); Jersey-club-influenced rap 130–140 with the 5-kick "bed-squeak" pattern; plugg / pluggnb 140–160 with sparse airy drums; dark R&B / trap-soul 60–75 (or 120–150 double-time); Afro-influenced R&B 95–110 with log-drum-like bass and shaker swing; Y2K R&B revival 88–100 on a swung 16th grid; West-coast / hyphy-leaning rap 96–104 with a clap-on-2-and-4 bounce.
- **Drum programming habits:** hats are the melody now: triplet rolls, 32nd-note bursts, and open-hat offbeats carry the movement while the kick sits sparse. Snares/claps are layered (a snappy snare + wide clap + a short reverb tail) and usually land late by 5–15 ms for a lazy pocket. Percussion is minimal: one shaker or one wood-tick, never a kit of toys. Radio drums are *mixed drier* than SoundCloud drums.
- **808 behavior:** the 808 is the lead instrument in trap and drill, a supporting instrument in R&B. Trap 808s are long, tuned to the root, saturated so they read on a phone, and ride a pattern that answers the kick rather than doubling it. Drill 808s glide (portamento slides into the fifth and the octave). Detroit/Flint 808s are pitched bass melodies played in 8th-note runs. R&B 808s are short, round, sub-only, one note per chord.
- **Harmonic habits:** 4-bar or 8-bar loops, two to four chords, minor keys dominate (natural minor and harmonic minor; Dorian for R&B and Afro lanes; Phrygian for drill and hard trap). Radio rap beats almost never modulate; R&B records may lift a half-step for the last section. The loop is usually a single lead melody line over a pad, not stacked chords.
- **Arrangement lengths:** radio edits run 2:15–3:15; the intro is 4–8 bars max (2 bars of melody alone, then drums). The full drum pattern must be in by 0:12. Instrumental sections repeat with subtraction (drop the hats, mute the 808 for two bars) rather than addition.
- **Beat switches are standard, not exotic.** A large share of 2025–2026 rap singles carry one mid-song switch: tempo halves or doubles, the 808 pattern or tuning changes, the melody is replaced entirely, or drums swap from trap hats to a drill/Jersey pattern. On radio the switch lands at ~1:45–2:15, is announced by two beats of silence or a half-bar of drums-only, and the second beat is shorter (16–24 bars).
- **Mix and master targets:** integrated loudness around -8 to -9 LUFS for trap/drill, -9 to -11 LUFS for R&B; true peak at -1.0 dBTP; high-pass around 30–35 Hz then shape 40–80 Hz for the 808's body; mono below ~120 Hz; sub-bass held in the center, hats and pads wide; a mid-forward, slightly dark tonal balance (radio processors add top end). Harmonic saturation on the 808 so it survives phone speakers.
- **Radio vs SoundCloud, in one sentence each:** radio is dry drums, one clear lead melody, tuned and saturated 808, a real arrangement with subtraction and a switch, mastered clean; SoundCloud is washed-out reverb, four stacked melodies, clipped 808 with no tuning, a two-minute loop with no arrangement.
- **Texture currency:** the rage-adjacent distorted synth lead has crossed into mainstream trap; R&B has moved to detuned analog pads, electric-guitar plucks and Y2K-style layered keys; Afro-influenced R&B brought log-drum bass and shaker swing to the R&B chart; Jersey club's kick pattern is now a rap drum option, not a novelty.
- **What the program director listens for:** does the first 5 seconds identify the record (a signature anomaly, see section 4), does the 808 hit on a car system without smearing, does the loop survive 3 minutes, is the drum pocket human, is there one thing nobody else's beat has.
- **Instrumental-specific:** since there's no vocal, the lead melody must sit where the vocal would (roughly 300 Hz–3 kHz), the hook section needs a countermelody or an octave lift so the ear registers a "chorus", and every 8 bars something must change (a hat pattern, a bass note, a texture) or the record reads as a loop.

## 6. Arrangement templates for instrumentals

### Template A — "Radio Melodic Trap" (2:45 at 140 BPM, ~96 bars)
- **Intro (bars 1–4):** lead melody alone over pad; anomaly signature strikes once on bar 1.
- **Verse 1 (5–20):** full drums enter on bar 5 with 808; hats in triplet rolls every fourth bar; anomaly recurs every 8th bar.
- **Hook 1 (21–36):** countermelody enters an octave up; open hat pattern; 808 slides on phrase ends; a half-beat silence before bar 21.
- **Verse 2 (37–52):** hats drop out for bars 37–40, return with a 32nd burst; pad narrows.
- **Hook 2 (53–68):** same as Hook 1 plus a second pluck layer.
- **Beat switch (69–84):** two beats of silence, tempo halves to a trap-soul pocket, same melody re-voiced on chorused keys, 808 becomes short and round.
- **Outro (85–96):** drums thin to kick and 808, melody rings out, final anomaly on the last bar.

### Template B — "Drill / Sexy Drill Radio" (2:30 at 142 BPM, ~88 bars)
- **Intro (1–8):** sliding 808 and strings alone; anomaly on bar 4 and bar 8.
- **Verse 1 (9–24):** drill kick grid enters; hats alternate triplet skips every bar.
- **Hook (25–40):** guitar or keys hook enters over the strings; 808 glides doubled; clap with plate tail.
- **Verse 2 (41–56):** kick dropout for one bar at 48; retriggered snare across beat 4 on 56.

## 9. Suno v6 settings

Context: v6 is the only model family available since 9 September 2026 (v6, v6-wild, v6-mini). The style field holds 1,000 characters; Exclude Styles holds 1,000 characters. Variety rewrites style prompts when raised; at 0 you keep full control of your tags. Max Mode uses more compute for consistency at 2x credit cost and is recommended for long songs and style transfer.

### (a) Safe radio instrumental
- **Model:** v6 (flagship) — described by Suno as "reliable, precise"; it follows a dense style field most faithfully.
- **Weirdness:** 15–25% — stays near the prompt so the drums, 808 and loop behave like a radio record (community guides converge on ~20%).
- **Style Influence:** 70–80% — strong enough to enforce the sub-lane and instrument list without the maximum pushing toward caricature.
- **Variety:** 0 (Off) — Suno's FAQ says Variety adjusts your style prompts; at 0 the generator's carefully written phrases are not rewritten.
- **Max Mode:** On — consistency across a 2:15–3:15 arrangement with a beat switch is exactly what it is designed for; worth the 2x cost for radio candidates.
- **Instrumental:** On, plus the Exclude list above, plus [Instrumental] in the lyrics box.

### (b) Experimental-but-radio
- **Model:** v6-wild for the first pass, then re-run winners on v6 — Wild is "less predictable and more varied", useful for discovering anomaly signatures and switches, but v6 gives the clean radio finish.
- **Weirdness:** 35–50% — enough to invent unexpected textures and rhythm edits without leaving the genre (Suno calls 50% the normal expected result).
- **Style Influence:** 60–70% — loosen slightly so Wild can improvise around the vocabulary.
- **Variety:** 0–1 — keep at 0 when the anomaly signature is written explicitly; a single notch adds surprise if generations are too samey.
- **Max Mode:** On for the v6 re-run; optional on Wild passes to save credits while exploring.
- **v6-mini:** use only for high-volume sketching of sub-lane ideas; it is the fastest and free tier, not the finish.
