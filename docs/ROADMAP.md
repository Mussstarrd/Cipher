## 1. What shipped today: the Groove Kernel (v0.1)

Built first because it is the one system that decides whether this game exists.
If the beat-built world doesn't groove in gray boxes, no amount of neon saves it.

**What's real in it**
- **The Conductor** — a sample-accurate beat clock on the Web Audio DSP timeline
  with tempo anchoring, the direct analogue of Unity's `AudioSettings.dspTime`.
  Gameplay is authored in *beats*, never seconds. Tempo ramps 88 → 100 BPM
  across a run and the world stays locked to it.
- **A synthesized boom-bap record** — kick, snare, hats, bass, sample chops and
  an erhu-ish lead, all generated by DSP with 58% swing on the 16ths. Zero audio
  assets, zero clearance risk. It proves the stem architecture end to end.
- **THE BUILD** — the core hook. Three on-beat clears unmute the next stem;
  a hit mutes one; a hit on a bare kick stops the record. The master filter
  opens as the mix grows, so the game gets *brighter* as you get better.
- **Three verbs** — Rise / Sink / Flow Stance, graded against the grid at
  ±70 ms (Perfect) and ±155 ms (On Time).
- **The chunk stitcher** — hand-authored one-bar phrases tagged by intensity,
  stitched procedurally against the energy curve. The real content pipeline in
  miniature.
- Wax fragments, districts, chain scoring, God Flow, and a record-stop death.

## 2. What the prototype taught us (findings, not opinions)

Three things the GDD got wrong or under-specified. This is what a kernel is for.

1. **Flow Stance cannot be slow-motion.** The GDD called for a slow-mo focus
   window. Slow-mo and a fixed beat grid are mutually exclusive — you cannot
   bend time without desyncing the track, and the track is the level. Flow
   Stance is now *breath control*: you phase through drone swarms at full
   tempo, with the master filter clamping down and afterimages trailing. It
   reads better and it protects the thing that makes the game special.
   **Sync is sacred. Nothing bends it.**

2. **The player needs notation.** Obstacles alone don't teach the grid. Adding a
   beat ruler on the tar — a tick per beat, tall and gold on the downbeat, plus
   a contact line at the player — made the bar readable before it arrives. Keep
   it in the Unity build; it is the difference between "unfair" and "I was late."

3. **The input is half a beat early, and that's correct.** Your move must *land*
   on the drum, not start on it. Jump airtime is tuned to 1.05 beats so the apex
   sits exactly on the contact beat. Players learn the anticipation in about
   four bars, and it's the skill that separates a chain from a clear. Onboarding
   must teach it explicitly in the first run.

2D animator, 3D environment artist, UI designer, audio director (+ contracted
producers), producer/LiveOps.

## 4. What I need decided to keep the schedule

- **Audio director + first three producers under contract by early Oct.** The
  music is not dressing, it is the level designer. Stems must be delivered
  aligned, at fixed BPM, with a documented manifest. This is the single longest
  lead time on the board and the one that will slip if we wait.
- **Unity 6 LTS confirmed** and a mid-tier Android test device (Snapdragon 665
  class) on every desk from day one. We do not profile at the end.
- **Art bible for Gousetsu City** — hand-inked 2D characters over low-poly 3D,
  charcoal and rust with iron gold / jade neon / sodium orange. Needed to start
  M2 art on time.

## 5. Risks, named honestly

- **Audio latency on low-end Android** is the top technical risk. Mitigation:
  AAudio low-latency path, per-device calibration, and a player-facing offset
  slider in options. Budget two engineer-weeks for this in M1 and do not skip it.
- **Content throughput.** 36 tracks × stems × beat-maps is the real cost of this
  design. The editor tool in M1 is not optional — it is what makes M3 possible.
- **Difficulty legibility.** Self-balancing difficulty (weak players get a
  sparse, readable track) is elegant but hides the failure state. Watch D1
  funnels in soft launch specifically for players who never reach stem 2.
