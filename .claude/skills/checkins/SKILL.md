# The four check-ins

07:00 · 12:00 · 17:00 · 22:00 America/New_York.

Four a day is a lot of contact. It only survives if each one has a **different
job** — four copies of the same digest is how people learn to ignore all of them.
Each check-in below answers a question the others do not.

## Before any of them

Read `memory/rhythms.md` first (anything inside its lead time), then
`open-loops.md`, `facts.md`, and the tail of yesterday's daily file. Then the
calendar. Memory first, calendar second — the calendar is what everyone can
already see.

## 07:00 — The day ahead

**Question: what does today need from us?**

Lead with the thing most likely to go wrong, not the first event chronologically.

- Today per person — Jeffery, Suzan, Aiden, Abby. Only what is theirs.
- Real departure times, adjusted for what has been learned about this family's
  actual clock, not the times written on the calendar.
- Anything needing a decision or a signature *today*.
- Rhythms inside their lead time — soccer registration, a bill landing before a
  paycheck, a renewal.
- Weather only if it changes a plan. Not a forecast.

## 12:00 — The midday adjust

**Question: what has changed since this morning?**

The shortest of the four. Often two lines. Skip entirely if nothing has moved and
say so rather than padding.

- What arrived since 07:00 that matters — school email, a cancellation, a
  reschedule.
- What is still unclaimed for this afternoon — a pickup nobody has confirmed.
- Anything now at risk that was fine at breakfast.

## 17:00 — The evening run

**Question: what has to happen before bed?**

The most operational one. This is the busiest hour in a house with a 9-year-old
and a 2-year-old, and it is where things get dropped.

- Dinner — what was planned, whether it still works given how the day went, and
  what to defrost or pick up if it doesn't.
- Tonight's logistics — practice, dance, whose car, what time to leave.
- **Paper due tomorrow** — forms to sign, money to send, kit to pack. Anything
  from `paper-trail` with a deadline of tomorrow surfaces here, not at 22:00 when
  it is too late to find a pen.
- Homework and bedtime-adjacent commitments.

## 22:00 — The close-out

**Question: is tomorrow going to work?**

Short and human-facing. Then, separately and silently, the evening review runs.

- Tomorrow's shape in three lines.
- Anything that must be signed, decided or packed before morning.
- Open loops that have gone quiet for over a week — one line, no nagging.
- What Hearth is unsure about, so it can be corrected before it matters.

**After sending it, run `.claude/skills/evening-review`.** That pass is not part
of this message and nobody sees it.

## Rules for all four

- **Phone-sized.** If a check-in takes more than thirty seconds to read it will
  stop being read, and an unread check-in is worse than none.
- **Never pad.** A quiet slot gets two lines or gets skipped. Manufacturing
  content to justify the schedule is how four-a-day becomes zero-a-day.
- **Specific over complete.** "Leave by 8:40 to make the 9:00" beats "don't
  forget school." Names, times, numbers.
- **Flag uncertainty rather than guessing.** "I think Aiden has practice — never
  confirmed" invites the correction that makes you right next week.
- **Track whether these get read.** If check-ins are consistently ignored, that
  goes in `misses.md` and the schedule gets cut. Four is what Jeffery asked for,
  not what is proven right.
